The problem

Technology for technology's sake is an expensive habit.

Technology buying goes wrong in two directions. Some businesses buy the cheapest thing available and replace it in eighteen months. Others buy what the salesperson was keenest on and pay for capability they will never use.

Software is worse, because it is invisible. Licences bought for people who left. Premium plans for staff who need the basic one. Subscriptions renewing each year because nobody remembers what they are for.

The better question for every purchase is simple: what does this do for earnings or risk, and what is the lowest total cost over its life?

What it's worth

Every recurring cost is multiplied

A saving on something you pay for every year is worth several times its face value when the business is valued on a multiple of earnings.

  • It cuts waste. Right-sized licences and well-chosen equipment are recurring savings that go straight to earnings.
  • It extends useful life. Equipment bought for the job lasts longer and is cheaper to support.
  • It keeps you compliant. A clean licence position avoids an unpleasant surprise in an audit or in due diligence.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • You are buying equipment without a clear standard
  • You suspect you are paying for licences you do not use
  • New laptops take days to become usable
  • You have no record of what you own or when it is due for replacement

Probably not the right fit if

  • You only want the lowest price on a specific model with no advice or set-up
  • You need consumer electronics for home use
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is IT procurement?

It is the process of choosing, buying, setting up and tracking the technology a business needs, including hardware, software and licences.

Is it cheaper to buy through you than directly?

Often, because of partner pricing, but price is only part of it. The larger savings come from buying the right specification, avoiding unused licences and not paying staff to set equipment up.

Should we buy or lease equipment?

It depends on your cash position and how your accountant wants to treat it. Buying usually costs less over the life of the equipment. We set out both so you can decide with your finance team.

What happens to our old equipment?

We wipe it securely so that no business data leaves with it, and arrange responsible disposal or recycling.
