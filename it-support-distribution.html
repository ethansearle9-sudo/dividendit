IT support for distribution and wholesale

In distribution, an IT failure does not inconvenience you. It stops an order being placed, and your customer notices before you do.

Why distribution is different

The cost of an outage is not measured in downtime. It is measured in shelf space.

Take a food brand selling through the main grocery chains. An email or server outage means a customer cannot place an order. On the face of it, that is a picking slip processed tomorrow instead of today.

Follow it through and it is worse than that. The order is late, the product is not on the shelf, and the shopper buys a competitor instead. The retailer loses a sale. If it happens twice, the buyer starts asking whether there is a supplier whose system of delivery is more reliable. The business consequence runs far deeper than the operational one.

Distribution also runs on routine payments to a lot of suppliers, which is precisely the environment invoice fraud is built for. Someone reads a mailbox quietly for weeks, waits for a real invoice, and sends a convincing request to change bank details. It is the single most expensive thing that routinely happens to businesses in this sector.

And the systems that matter are rarely just email. Stock, ERP, EDI links into customers, warehouse scanners and the integrations between them all have to keep working together.

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest IT risk for a distribution business?

Payment diversion, by a distance. Criminals get into a mailbox, read supplier conversations, and at the right moment send a convincing request to change bank details. A genuine invoice gets paid into a criminal account. It is far more common than ransomware and it targets exactly the routine supplier payments distribution runs on.

Do you support ERP and EDI systems?

We keep the environment they run on healthy and we own the relationship with the vendors who support the applications themselves. We are not an ERP developer. What we do is make sure the servers, connections, backups and access controls underneath are right, and that when something breaks somebody is chasing it rather than you.

Our warehouse starts at 5am. Does support cover that?

Yes. Cover outside office hours is the point of a team rather than one person. Despatch failing at five in the morning is a normal incident in this sector, not an edge case.

We already have an IT person. Is this still relevant?

Usually yes, as co-managed. They keep the relationship with the business and stop being the only one who understands the integrations. You get cover for leave and out of hours and stop carrying single-person risk.

What does it cost?

Per user, per month, tools included, depending on headcount and what is in place. Twenty minutes gives an honest number, and contracts roll monthly.
