The problem

When a server stops, so does the business.

A laptop failing is one person's bad day. A server failing can stop the whole company: the accounting system, the stock system, the files everyone needs.

Servers are also easy to neglect, because when they work nobody thinks about them. Updates get postponed because restarting is inconvenient. Disks fill slowly. The warranty quietly expires. Backups report success for a job that is no longer backing up the right things.

Good server support is mostly unglamorous discipline: watching, patching, checking and planning, so that the dramatic day never comes.

What it's worth

Own it or rent it: a decision worth making on purpose

For systems that do the same job every day, a well-run server you own can cost far less over its life than renting the same capacity in the cloud.

  • It protects revenue. Fewer outages on the systems your business trades on.
  • It can improve earnings. Owned equipment is capitalised and depreciated. Rented capacity is expensed every month. Our own vs rent analysis shows when each wins.
  • It removes a diligence finding. Out-of-support servers and undocumented systems are among the first things a buyer's adviser flags.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • Your accounting, stock or production system runs on a server
  • You are not sure when your servers were last updated
  • Your server hardware is more than five years old
  • You are being told to move everything to the cloud and want a second opinion

Probably not the right fit if

  • Everything you use is already delivered as software from the cloud
  • You want hardware supplied with no ongoing management
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What does managed server support include?

Continuous monitoring, planned patching, backup verification, capacity planning, hardware lifecycle tracking and documentation, with engineers who respond when something needs attention.

Do we still need servers, or should everything move to the cloud?

It depends on the workload. Email and files usually belong in the cloud. Steady, predictable business systems are often cheaper to run on equipment you own. We look at it workload by workload, with the numbers on the table.

How do you patch servers without disrupting the business?

Updates are tested first and applied in an agreed maintenance window outside working hours, with a way back if something goes wrong.

Can you manage cloud servers as well?

Yes. We manage servers wherever they run, whether in your office, in a data centre or with a public cloud provider.
