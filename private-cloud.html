Private cloud: own the steady

Your core business systems do roughly the same work every day. Renting that capacity forever is a choice, and usually the expensive one. Private cloud means your own servers, professionally hosted and fully managed, capitalised and depreciated across five to seven years instead of expensed every month for ever.

The problem

The quietest way a business erodes its own earnings.

Public cloud is built for workloads that change: fast growth, seasonal peaks, demand you cannot forecast. Most established businesses do not run workloads like that. Their accounting, stock and operational systems do about the same amount of work this month as last.

Renting capacity for those systems means paying a premium for flexibility you never use. And because rent is expensed, it reduces earnings every month, the figure your valuation is built on.

A distribution group our founder advised compared the two options for its core business system. Buying a server cost about €7,500 once. Renting the same capacity cost about €1,250 a month. Owning paid for itself in six months, and the server ran for seven years.

What it's worth

Payback in months. Value for years.

The software company 37signals publicly documented leaving the cloud: an annual bill of about $3.2 million replaced largely by about $700,000 of owned servers, for savings it put at around $2 million a year. At small business scale the shape is the same.

  • It adds to earnings. Once the hardware has paid for itself, every month after is earnings kept and not rent paid.
  • It multiplies at exit. A recurring saving is worth several times its annual value when the business is valued on a multiple.
  • It gives up very little. What you lose is the ability to scale overnight, which a steadily growing business rarely needs.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • Your core systems do roughly the same work every month
  • Your cloud or hosting bill is a significant monthly cost
  • You expect to own the business for several more years, or to sell it on earnings
  • You want to know exactly where your data is held

Probably not the right fit if

  • Your demand is unpredictable or highly seasonal. Public cloud suits that better
  • You are a very early-stage business whose needs will change quickly
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is a private cloud?

A private cloud is server infrastructure dedicated to one business, as opposed to capacity shared with other customers. Ours is equipment you own, hosted in a professional data centre and managed for you.

Is private cloud cheaper than public cloud?

For steady, predictable workloads it usually is, often by a wide margin over the life of the hardware. For workloads that vary a great deal, public cloud is usually better. We compare both with your real numbers.

Who looks after the hardware?

We do. Monitoring, updates, security, backup and hardware faults are all handled as part of the service, with manufacturer warranty behind the equipment.

What happens when the hardware reaches the end of its life?

Replacement is planned from the start. We track warranty and capacity and budget for renewal well ahead, so it is a scheduled project and not an emergency.

Is it as reliable as the big cloud providers?

Hosted in a Tier III data centre with resilient power and connectivity, and with proper backup and disaster recovery, it is designed for high availability. We agree the level of resilience your business needs and build to that.
