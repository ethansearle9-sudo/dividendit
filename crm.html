The problem

The graveyard of failed CRMs is a training problem, not a software problem.

Most businesses that say their CRM failed chose a perfectly good product. What failed was everything around it: it was configured for a generic sales process and not theirs, the old data came across in a mess, and after the first month nobody was asked to use it.

So the business went back to what it had before. Relationships in individual inboxes. A spreadsheet only one person understands. Forecasts built on gut feel.

That is a commercial problem today and a valuation problem later. When a key salesperson leaves, the relationships leave with them.

What it's worth

Buyers pay for pipelines they can verify

In due diligence, “strong customer relationships” is a claim. A CRM with years of clean pipeline history is evidence.

  • It proves recurring revenue. A buyer can see customer history, renewal patterns and concentration for themselves.
  • It reduces dependence on individuals. Relationships belong to the business and not to whoever holds the inbox.
  • It sharpens the forecast. Decisions are made on what the pipeline shows and not on optimism.

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What does a CRM consultant do?

A CRM consultant helps a business choose the right customer relationship management system, sets it up around the way the business sells, moves existing data across and helps the team adopt it.

Which CRM is best for a small business?

There is no single answer. It depends on your sales process, team size, budget and the other systems it has to connect to. Because we do not resell any CRM, we can recommend whichever fits.

Why do CRM implementations fail?

Rarely because of the software. The common causes are a set-up that does not match how the team sells, poor quality data, and no follow-through on adoption after launch.

How long does a CRM implementation take?

It depends on the complexity of your sales process and the state of your data. We agree a realistic plan in advance, and usually launch a simple version first and build from there.

Can you fix a CRM we already have?

Often, yes. If the product is sound, we would sooner reconfigure it, clean the data and relaunch it properly than have you pay for a new one.
