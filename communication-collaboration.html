The problem

Five tools, five suppliers, and nothing quite connects.

How quickly your team communicates decides how quickly the business moves. Yet in most companies the tools arrived one at a time: email from one place, phones from another, a file server in the cupboard, a CRM somebody championed and a video licence bought in a hurry.

Each has its own supplier, its own bill and its own login. Calls are not logged against customers. Files live in three places. When something fails, each supplier blames the next.

The technology is not the hard part. The hard part is making deliberate choices about what runs where, and having one team accountable for it working together.

What it's worth

Rent what flexes. Own what runs steady.

The industry's default answer is to move everything to the cloud. The right answer is decided workload by workload, with the effect on earnings on the table.

  • It removes duplicate spend. Overlapping tools and unused licences are recurring costs that come straight off earnings.
  • It speeds the business up. Calls, files and customer history in one place means less searching and less re-typing.
  • It builds value. Systems that hold your customer relationships and know-how are part of what a buyer pays for.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • Your team works across office, home and client sites
  • You pay several suppliers for overlapping tools
  • Your phone system or file server is ageing
  • Customer information lives in inboxes and spreadsheets

Probably not the right fit if

  • You want a single product licence with no set-up or support
  • You need tools for personal or family use
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What are communication and collaboration tools?

They are the systems a team uses to talk and work together: email, messaging, video meetings, phones, shared files and customer records. Microsoft 365 and Teams, a cloud phone system and a CRM are the usual core.

Should we use Microsoft Teams for our phones?

Often it is a good fit, because calls then live where your people already work. It depends on how you handle calls. Businesses with reception desks, queues or heavy call volumes sometimes do better with a dedicated cloud phone system that integrates with Teams.

Do we have to move everything to the cloud?

No. Email and collaboration belong in the cloud. Steady business systems are often cheaper on infrastructure you own. We decide workload by workload. Our own vs rent analysis explains how.

Can you take over the tools we already have?

Yes. We take on existing systems as they are, tidy up licensing and security, and only recommend changes where there is a clear benefit.
