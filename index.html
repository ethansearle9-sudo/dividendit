From the field

Three deals that taught us the doctrine.

A decade inside an outsourced IT & cybersecurity business, sitting across the table from CFOs and owners. Names withheld — the numbers and the lessons are real.

Distribution & ManufacturingPost management buyout
Later exited to trade

"Move everything to the cloud — it's cheaper and I won't have to worry."

The CFO opened with the industry-standard answer. It was wrong on both counts. We priced the core workload honestly: roughly €8,000 of owned compute, hosted in a Tier-III data centre, replaced €1,350 a month of rented cloud compute. Payback in under six months. The server ran seven years.

Email and files went to Microsoft 365 — where SaaS genuinely earns its place. Everything else was placed deliberately. Same uptime as a hyperscaler. None of the expensed-compute premium. The business later exited to a trade buyer.

~6 mo
payback on owned compute — then every month after was EBITDA retained, not rent paid
Medical DistributionPositioning for exit
Acquired within the year

The business that priced every decision twice.

Every recommendation we brought was assessed the same way: first the saving, then the saving multiplied by their exit multiple. A licence optimisation was never a €5,000 decision — at their multiple it was a €30,000 enterprise value decision.

Right-sized licensing for frontline staff and perpetual purchases where the maths favoured them added up to six figures of value that would otherwise have leaked into recurring cost. They were acquired roughly a year later.

× multiple
the discipline: every recurring cost removed compounds into the valuation
Financial ServicesWealth management
Clients worldwide

Funding idle compute while the value engine sat under-defended.

Their value engine is people — advisers building client trust over decades. Yet every workload sat in expensed public cloud, eating budget, while the advisers themselves — working from every timezone, carrying sensitive client data — were the actual risk surface.

We rebalanced: right-sized the compute, moved the security spend to where the risk actually sat. Total IT cost went down. Risk profile improved. The regulatory audit trail got stronger.
