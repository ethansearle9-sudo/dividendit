IT support for medical practices and life sciences

Health data carries the strictest obligations in data protection law, and most practices and suppliers are running it on systems nobody has reviewed in years. Why medical is different

Every other sector asks what a breach would cost. Here you also have to ask who you must tell.

Health data is not ordinary personal data. Under GDPR it is special category data, which carries higher obligations on how it is held and who may see it, and a breach brings notification duties that arrive on a clock rather than when convenient.

That applies whether you are a practice holding patient records, a device distributor whose systems touch clinical information, or a supplier into pharma and medtech. The obligation follows the data, not the size of the business.

Suppliers into the sector have a second problem. Regulated customers push their requirements down the chain, so a supplier security questionnaire eventually lands asking about access control, backup testing and incident response. For a business with no IT person, that document is a fire drill, and a weak answer can quietly cost a contract.

There is also the practical reality. Practice management systems, imaging, and the specialist clinical software this sector runs on are often older, supported by a single vendor, and treated as untouchable. Untouchable is how systems end up unpatched for years.

This is one of the industries we work in. The others, and what is different about each, are on our managed IT services by industry page.

How it works

How we start with a medical business

Find where clinical data actually lives

Usually in more places than expected: the practice system, email, shared drives and somebody's laptop.

Tighten access

Limit who can reach it, enforce multi-factor sign-in, and review the list properly.

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

Does GDPR treat health data differently?

Yes. Health data is special category data, which means stricter conditions on holding and processing it and higher expectations of the security around it. A breach involving it also brings notification obligations that run to a deadline from the point you become aware.

Our practice management system is old and the vendor says do not touch it. What then?

That is common and it is workable. Where a clinical system genuinely cannot be patched or changed, it gets isolated so a compromise elsewhere cannot reach it, access to it is tightly controlled, and it is monitored. Pretending it is fine because the vendor says so is the version that goes wrong.

We supply into pharma and medtech. Can you help with their questionnaires?

Yes, and it is a common reason businesses in this sector call. We make sure the controls being asked about are genuinely in place and keep the evidence, so the questionnaire becomes an administrative task.

Do you work with veterinary and animal health businesses?

Yes. The commercial shape is very similar: clinical records, practice systems, specialist software and distribution into a regulated chain.

What does it cost?

Per user, per month, tools included, depending on headcount and what is in place. Twenty minutes gives an honest number, and contracts roll monthly.
