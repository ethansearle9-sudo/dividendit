M&A cyber due diligence

When you buy a business, you buy its security history and its weaknesses along with it. Cyber due diligence shows you what you are really acquiring, and what it will cost to put right. You get the material risks, a remediation cost and an integration plan, early enough to still shape the deal.

The problem

Cyber problems found after completion are yours.

Financial and legal diligence are routine. Cyber diligence is still often a paragraph in a report, based on a questionnaire the seller filled in. That leaves real exposure unexamined.

An undisclosed breach, customer data held insecurely, years of missed updates, software nobody is licensed for. Each is a liability that transfers to the buyer on completion, and each could have changed the price or the warranties had it been known.

The risk runs both ways. If you are the seller, the gaps a buyer's adviser finds become reasons to chip the price or delay. Finding and fixing them first keeps you in control of the conversation.

What we examine

✓

Security posture review

A technical and governance review of the target's controls: sign-in, devices, backup, network and monitoring.

✓

Incident and breach history

What has happened before, how it was handled and whether there are signs of something current and undiscovered.

✓

Data and regulatory exposure

What personal and sensitive data is held, how it is protected and where obligations may not be met.

✓

Remediation cost

A realistic figure for bringing the business up to standard, so it can be reflected in price or terms.

✓

Integration risk

What it would take to connect the target's systems to yours safely, and what to keep apart until it is fixed.

✓

A post-completion roadmap

A practical plan for the first six to twelve months after the deal.

What it's worth

A number, not a worry

Deals are priced on earnings and risk. Cyber diligence makes one of the least visible risks concrete enough to price.

  • For buyers: avoid overpaying, and secure the right warranties and price adjustments.
  • For sellers: fix issues before a buyer finds them, and present evidence that supports your valuation.
  • For both: a faster process, because the questions have answers.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • You are acquiring or investing in a business
  • You plan to sell in the next one to three years and want to be ready
  • Your advisers have asked for a specialist view on technology risk
  • The target holds significant customer or personal data

Probably not the right fit if

  • The deal has already completed. A risk assessment is the right tool now
  • You need legal or financial due diligence. We work alongside those advisers and do not replace them
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is cyber due diligence?

It is an assessment of a company's cyber security risk carried out as part of a merger, acquisition or investment, so that weaknesses, past incidents and remediation costs are understood before the deal completes.

When should cyber due diligence happen?

As early as the deal allows, and before terms are final, so that the findings can shape price, warranties and conditions.

We are selling. Why would we commission it ourselves?

Because you get to find and fix issues on your own timetable instead of having them discovered by the buyer's adviser at the worst moment. A seller who presents clean evidence is negotiating from strength.

How is this different from IT due diligence?

IT due diligence looks at the whole technology estate: systems, costs, contracts, licences and how hard integration will be. Cyber due diligence goes deep on security risk and incident history. We often run both together. See M&A IT due diligence.
