The Galway problem

Supplying a regulated customer makes you answerable to their standards, not yours.

Galway's economy has a centre of gravity: medical devices, and the long tail of businesses that supply, machine, package, transport and service that sector. Parkmore and Ballybrit are full of them.

That is good business, and it comes with a condition. Regulated customers push their obligations down the supply chain. Sooner or later a supplier security questionnaire lands, asking about multi-factor sign-in, backup testing, access control and incident response. For a 40-person business with no IT person, that document is a fire drill, and a poor answer can cost the contract.

There is a second effect. Suppliers to a cluster are worth attacking precisely because they are the softer route into a larger, better-defended customer. Being small is not cover.

What is needed is not a compliance project. It is having the controls genuinely running, with the evidence sitting there when somebody asks.

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

Do you come on site in Galway?

Yes. Most issues are resolved remotely and faster that way. When hardware needs hands we come out, across the city, Parkmore, Ballybrit, Oranmore and towards Tuam.

Can you help with supplier security questionnaires?

Yes, and it is a common reason businesses here call. We make sure the controls being asked about are genuinely in place, and we keep the evidence, so completing one becomes an administrative task rather than a project.

Do you do ISO 27001 certification?

No. We do not offer ISO 27001 certification or readiness work. We structure security around the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and report against it, which answers most supplier questionnaires, but if you need certified compliance you need a specialist and we will say so.

Are small Galway suppliers really targeted?

Yes, and often because of who you sell to rather than who you are. Attacking a smaller supplier is a common route into a larger, better-defended customer. Invoice fraud and mailbox takeover are far more frequent than ransomware.

What does it cost?

Per user, per month, tools included, depending on headcount and what is already in place. Twenty minutes gives an honest number. Contracts roll monthly.
