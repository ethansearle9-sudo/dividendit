## Video: Understanding the E-E-A-T framework

Experience, Expertise, Authority, and Trustworthiness — EEAT — is the framework Google uses to evaluate the quality and credibility of content. It has been part of Google's quality evaluator guidelines for years, and it has become even more critical in the age of AI-powered search. AI answer engines like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity don't just look for relevant content. They look for content they can trust, and EEAT is how they decide.

**Experience.** Whether your content reflects genuine, hands-on involvement with the topic. Did you actually work through the problem you're describing? Have you seen it play out in real campaigns or with real customers? A blog post about improving email open rates using data from a campaign you actually ran carries more credibility than a post that rehashes general advice from other articles. Algorithms are getting better at distinguishing content that comes from lived practice versus content assembled second-hand. Original examples, real-world outcomes, and an honest perspective from your own work are what demonstrate experience.

**Expertise.** The depth and credibility of the knowledge behind your content — whether the person or organisation producing it has the background to speak with authority. In sensitive fields like medicine or finance this is especially scrutinised, but it matters everywhere. A post that includes a detailed author bio highlighting relevant years of experience and a link to a LinkedIn profile signals that this isn't just an opinion; it's an informed perspective. **Content tied to a credible, visible author carries significantly more weight.**

**Authority.** Where expertise is about the individual, authority is about your broader reputation across the web — how other sources perceive and reference your brand. When your content gets cited in an industry newsletter or linked by a well-known publication, that's authority in action. Those third-party references signal to AI engines that your company is a reliable source worth citing, consistently.

**Trustworthiness.** What holds the framework together. Trust is built on transparency: who wrote the content, where the information comes from, whether the site is secure, and whether the brand is upfront about what it does. Every post has a clear author, every statistic links to its source, the site has an up-to-date privacy policy. None of it is flashy, but all of it builds the foundation. **When trust is missing, even strong experience and expertise won't be enough to earn visibility.**

EEAT isn't a checklist you complete once. It's a reflection of how your entire content practice is built. AI answer engines scan the whole web when deciding which sources to cite. A consistent, authoritative, transparent presence across your website, your social channels and your guest content compounds over time.

## Video: How E-E-A-T informs a balanced SEO and AEO strategy

SEO and AEO are not competing priorities. They share a foundation, and that foundation is EEAT.

One distinction worth understanding: **SEO is largely about earning high rankings across search results. AEO goes a step further — it's about being selected as the single most credible answer to a question**, whether that's a featured snippet in Google or a citation in ChatGPT or Perplexity. In that environment, EEAT isn't just helpful; it's the deciding factor.

**Experience.** For SEO, experience-driven content tends to be more detailed, specific and useful, and search engines reward it because it keeps users engaged. For AEO the stakes are higher: AI answer engines are designed to surface content that reflects genuine understanding, not recycled information. A post about a campaign that includes the real decisions made along the way and specific results is harder to replicate and easier for algorithms to trust.

**Expertise.** SEO has always rewarded content that goes deep. Shallow content might rank briefly but rarely holds position. AEO raises the bar: AI tools pull from sources they consider knowledgeable on a topic. If you consistently publish accurate, detailed content in your area, clearly tied to people with real credentials, your brand becomes a reference point. **One practical signal: every article should be attributed to a named author with a bio reflecting relevant experience, roles and accomplishments.**

**Authority.** In SEO, authority is largely built through backlinks. In AEO the broader view matters even more: **AI engines look at whether your brand is referenced, mentioned and recommended — not just linked to.** Guest contributions, industry mentions and recognition in curated newsletters all contribute.

**Trust.** For SEO: a secure site, accurate and current content, clear ways for users to get help. For AEO, trust is non-negotiable — AI engines are built to avoid surfacing unreliable content. A site that's transparent about who produces its content, cites its sources, and keeps information current is far more likely to be selected as a cited source. Keep author bios current, fact-check every published statistic, and link out to original sources wherever possible.

Both reward high-quality credible content. Both value clear authorship and sourcing. Both are strengthened by a strong brand reputation. When you invest in one you're often strengthening the other.

## Video: Where are you in the hierarchy of AEO needs?

With so many tactics and best practices out there, it's easy to feel you need to do everything at once. The most effective AEO strategies aren't built that way. They're built in layers, each one creating the conditions for the next to succeed — like Maslow's hierarchy. **Understanding where you currently sit is what determines where you should focus your energy right now.**

**Level 1 — Crawlability and indexability.** Before AI engines can cite your content they need to find and process it. The site must be technically sound: fast, secure, mobile-friendly, free of errors that prevent access. If this foundation isn't solid, everything above it is at risk.

**Level 2 — Content quality.** Once your site can be found, the question becomes "is there anything worth finding?" This is where EEAT does its heaviest lifting. Content needs to reflect real experience, demonstrate genuine expertise, and give readers accurate, well-sourced information. Superficial, generic or outdated content won't be selected, no matter how well the site performs technically.

**Level 3 — Answer-focused structure.** It's not enough to have good content; it needs to be structured so AI engines can parse and extract it. Organise content around the real questions your audience asks, lead with direct answers, and use clear formatting that makes key points easy to identify. Content that buries its main point or rambles before getting to the answer is less likely to be surfaced.

**Level 4 — Authority and off-site presence.** Once your content is high quality and well-structured, the next priority is credibility beyond your own website: backlinks from reputable sources, contributions to industry publications, brand mentions and references across the web.

**Level 5 — Optimization and refinement.** Fine-tuning. Track which content is being cited, which questions you're answering well, and where gaps remain. Update content regularly. Go deeper on the topics where your brand has the most authority and opportunity.

**How to find your level.** Can search engines and AI tools easily access and index your site? If no or unsure, that's your starting point. Is your content genuinely high quality, well-sourced and tied to credible authors? Is it structured around the questions your audience is actually asking? Are other reputable sources referencing your brand? Are you tracking performance and refining?

> **Investing in authority-building before your content quality is solid, or chasing optimization before your technical foundation is stable, won't get you the results you're looking for.**

You don't have to be at level five to see results, but you do need to be honest about where the gaps are.
