## Video: How search has evolved

When search engines first launched in the early 1990s they were essentially online directories. You typed a keyword and got back a list of links containing that keyword. The more times you used the keyword, the better your chances — which is where keyword stuffing came from, and why early results were often unhelpful.

**1998 — PageRank.** Links between websites could signal quality and authority. If other credible sites linked to your page, that was a vote of confidence. Search stopped being only about matching keywords and started being about which pages were trustworthy and useful.

**2011 — Panda** penalised thin and low-quality content. **2013 — Hummingbird** helped the engine understand the *intent* behind a query rather than the literal words. After Hummingbird, Google could recognise that "best running shoes for flat feet" was a request for a recommendation, not a list of pages containing those words.

**Featured snippets and knowledge panels** were an important turning point: for the first time Google pulled content directly from web pages and displayed it in the results. The search engine began acting less like a directory and more like an answer provider.

**Today — zero-click searches.** A significant and growing share of searches end without a click, because the engine provides the answer on the results page.

**AI Overviews are a much larger leap than featured snippets.** A featured snippet pulls a direct excerpt from a single page, quoting it word for word and linking back to that one source. AI Overviews are AI-generated summaries that use Gemini to pull from *multiple* web pages and synthesise an original response, blending several pages into one cohesive answer with citations.

> **This is the meaningful distinction for marketers. Featured snippets reward having the single best answer on a page. AI Overviews reward having content that's trustworthy and structured enough to be woven into a broader, multi-source response.** Rather than one page winning the top spot, multiple sources can now contribute to a single answer. That changes the competitive dynamic considerably.

And it isn't just Google. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and voice assistants answer questions directly too.

In traditional search the goal was to rank high enough that someone clicked your link. **In the age of answer engines the goal is increasingly whether an AI system trusts your content enough to pull from it, cite it, or recommend it.**

Traditional search isn't going away — Google still processes around 13 billion searches a day, so organic rankings still matter. But the definition of visibility has expanded.

## Video: What's an answer engine?

**An answer engine is any AI-powered system that responds to a query by generating a direct answer, rather than returning a list of links.** The key distinction is the output. A traditional search engine gives you a ranked list and leaves you to click through, read, compare and decide. An answer engine does much of that work for you: it processes information from across the web, synthesises it, and delivers a response.

**The platforms:**

- **Google AI Overviews** — AI-generated summaries at the top of Google results, pulling from multiple websites. Powered by Gemini. You get the core answer without clicking any source.
- **ChatGPT** — conversational AI from OpenAI. Depending on version, draws from training data alone or searches the web in real time, citing sources when it does.
- **Perplexity** — combines real-time web retrieval with AI-generated summaries and inline citations. Of the major answer engines, often the most transparent about where its information comes from, which makes it a useful reference point for understanding how AI citation works.
- **Claude** — conversational assistant from Anthropic. Same principle: synthesises information and delivers a direct answer rather than a list of links, so the same content principles apply.
- **Voice assistants** (Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant) also qualify — they respond with a single spoken answer, typically pulled from a featured snippet or AI summary.

**Three characteristics all of them share, and why each matters:**

1. **Answer engines prioritise direct answers.** The experience is built around delivering a response, not a reading list. **Your content needs to contain clear, extractable answers to be included in what these systems generate.**
2. **Answer engines evaluate sources for trust and authority.** They don't grab the first result. They assess whether a source is credible, whether the information is consistent with what other trusted sources say, and whether the content is structured in a way that makes it easy to interpret. This is where investment in SEO fundamentals — domain authority, backlinks, E-E-A-T signals, technical health — continues to pay off.
3. **Answer engines may or may not link back to you.** Perplexity and AI Overviews include citations; voice assistants typically deliver the answer with no attribution. **Being cited doesn't always drive a click, but it does contribute to brand visibility, credibility, and the perception that you're a trusted authority.**

The question changes from "how do I get someone to click my link?" to **"how do I make sure AI systems see my content as worth citing?"**

## Video: How answer engines work

At the core of most modern answer engines is a **large language model (LLM)**: AI trained on enormous amounts of text, identifying patterns in how words, phrases and ideas appear together. It learns by repeatedly predicting the next word in a passage, checking whether it got it right, and adjusting. Once trained, it uses that same prediction ability to generate responses.

Think of a research analyst who has spent years reading every report, white paper and case study in your industry. They couldn't recite any one document from memory, but they've built a strong working knowledge of the themes, terminology and arguments that recur. When you ask them a question they synthesise what they've absorbed into a clear, informed answer. That's essentially how an LLM generates a response — it draws on patterns across everything it's processed to produce something new, without quoting a single source directly.

**An important detail for marketers: not all LLMs pull from the web the same way.**

- **Real-time retrieval** — Perplexity and Google AI Overviews actively search the web when generating an answer. They retrieve current pages, evaluate them, synthesise from multiple sources, and often cite the specific pages they drew from.
- **Training data only** — base versions of ChatGPT rely primarily on what they learned during training rather than searching live. Answers can still be highly relevant but are based on patterns in training data.

A caution that applies both ways: training data can carry biases or present outdated information with the same confidence as current facts, and the internet retrieved in real time is not a neutral source either. AI answers aren't immune to errors, gaps or skewed perspectives.

**The key difference in how you're evaluated:**

In traditional search, an engine crawls your page, indexes it, ranks it against other pages on hundreds of signals, and the output is a *position*. Rank in the top three and you get clicks.

An answer engine instead scans available content for information relevant to the query. It evaluates whether your content is useful, trustworthy and clearly structured enough to extract from. If it is, your information gets woven into a generated response and may be cited. **If it isn't, your page may not appear at all — even if it ranks well in traditional search.**

> **Ranking is about earning a position on a page. Being cited is about earning a place in an answer.**

## Video: The evolution of SEO to AEO

**Answer engine optimisation (AEO) is the practice of structuring your content so AI-powered systems can find it, understand it, and cite it when generating direct answers.** It's a newer discipline with new tactics, but it doesn't require starting from scratch — it builds directly on top of SEO.

At the centre of an effective SEO strategy you need:

- High-quality content that demonstrates real expertise and answers real questions
- Pages structured with clear headers and logical organisation
- Authority built through backlinks, reputation, and consistent brand presence
- A technically sound site that is fast and easy to crawl

**These same principles are the foundation answer engines rely on when deciding which sources to trust and cite.** When an answer engine generates a response it evaluates sources across content clarity, domain reputation, consistency with other trusted sources, and how easily information can be extracted.

**Where the overlap shows up in practice:**

**E-E-A-T.** When an AI system synthesises an answer from multiple sources it has to determine which are credible enough to draw from. Content demonstrating genuine expertise — often in the form of original research — backed by credible authors and coming from an authoritative domain is more likely to be considered for citation.

**Content structure and clarity.** In traditional SEO, well-organised pages with clear headers help engines understand your content and match it to queries. For answer engines, structure matters for a different reason: direct answers, descriptive headers and logical section breaks help AI systems extract your content and weave it into a response. **In SEO, poor structure can hurt your rankings. In AEO, poor structure can mean your content gets passed over entirely, even if the information itself is strong.**

**Backlinks and brand mentions.** Backlinks still matter because they contribute to domain authority, which answer engines factor into trust assessments. But answer engines have their own, more direct measure: **brand mentions.** AI systems pay attention to whether your brand is mentioned consistently across independent sources — publications, forums, review sites, social platforms. **These mentions don't need to include a link to have a significant impact.** An answer engine scanning Reddit threads, blog posts and news articles is looking for consensus — whether multiple independent voices agree you're a credible player.

> **In traditional SEO, authority was primarily expressed through links. In AEO, authority is increasingly measured through mentions, citations, and the consistency of how your brand is represented across the web.**

**Structured data.** In SEO, schema markup helped pages qualify for rich results. In AEO it serves an expanded purpose: it makes your content easier for AI systems to read, interpret and use. It won't guarantee a citation, but it reduces the guesswork an answer engine has to do.

**Where AEO adds something genuinely new:**

1. **Format for extraction.** Lead with direct answers, use descriptive headers, and organise each section so it can stand on its own. If an AI system has to piece together scattered information from across a long page, it's more likely to pass over your content and pull from a source that's easier to work with.
2. **Prioritise freshness/recency.** Answer engines favour up-to-date information, so outdated content is less likely to be cited even if it was once a top performer. Regular review and updating becomes an active part of strategy.
3. **New measurement challenges.** Tracking whether your content was cited in an AI answer is fundamentally different from tracking a position on a results page, and the tools are still developing.

AEO doesn't replace SEO and doesn't require a separate strategy. The strongest strategies treat them as two sides of the same practice.
