# SERP reality check — Irish market, 13 Nov 2026

Ten target SERPs read directly on google.ie with `gl=ie`, following HubSpot
AEO Fundamentals lesson 4 ("read the SERP before you write"). Google Ads
Keyword Planner froze twice and was abandoned rather than thrashed, so there
are no absolute volume figures here — this is competitive structure and
intent, which is the part that changes decisions.

## Finding 1: every single SERP has an AI Overview

10 of 10. Head terms, location terms, vertical terms, cost terms, decision
terms, M&A terms — all of them.

Two consequences, and they pull in opposite directions:

- The AEO work just completed is aimed correctly. This is not a future
  problem; it is the present state of every query we care about.
- Organic clicks will be suppressed on all of them. **Being cited matters
  more than ranking**, and the measurement gap from lesson 6 applies to this
  site immediately, not eventually.

## Finding 2: the head terms are saturated

| Query | Established `.ie` competitors on page 1 |
|---|---|
| it support dublin | everythingit, it.ie, sweeneycomputers, ers, imageit, radium, orbit, texaport, techsure |
| managed it services dublin | ers, it.ie, everythingit, nostra, datatrust, twl + **clutch.co and cloudtango.net** |
| outsourced it support ireland | ers, rochfort, hcs, yellowcom, radium, unitec, imageit, landmark |
| it support limerick | paqit, scope, unitec, dbcomp, jkcs, radium, panoptic + goldenpages |

Each has 8+ incumbents with dedicated, mature pages. We are at average
position 57.6 with no authority. These are not winnable in the near term.

**Clutch and Cloudtango rank page one for "managed it services dublin"**,
which confirms the directory work: a profile there is a page-one asset we do
not have to build authority for ourselves. Golden Pages ranks for Limerick.

## Finding 3 — a correction

**I previously told Ethan that distribution, manufacturing and FMCG were
"uncontested". That was wrong.** It was based on scanning six named
competitors' service menus rather than reading the SERPs. The SERPs say
otherwise:

| Query | Competitors with dedicated vertical pages |
|---|---|
| it support for manufacturing ireland | unitec, spector, ers, iping, hybridtp, radium, landmark |
| it support for distribution companies ireland | unitec, panoptic, ers, hybridtp, infinitetechnology, apexit |

Seven and six respectively. The verticals are contested, and by providers
with more authority than us. The vertical pages are still worth having —
they are how we speak to a defined audience, which the checklist requires —
but they are not an open goal and should not be planned as one.

## Finding 4: two competitors are running our playbook already

`ers.ie` and `landmark.ie` appear on nearly every SERP tested, including the
cost and decision queries:

- landmark.ie ranks for *outsourced it support ireland*, *how much does
  outsourced it cost*, *do i need an it manager*, and *it support for
  manufacturing* — four of our intended wedges.
- ers.ie ranks for *it support dublin*, *managed it services dublin*,
  *outsourced it support ireland*, *cost*, *in-house vs managed*,
  *manufacturing* and *distribution*.

They publish dated, priced, Ireland-specific content ("Managed IT Services
Cost in Ireland: 2026 Price Guide", "In-House IT vs Managed IT in Ireland:
Cost Compared"). These two are the benchmark to study, not the six scanned
previously.

## Finding 5: where the Irish field is genuinely thin

This is the actionable part.

**`do i need an it manager or outsource`** — page one is mostly UK and US
(ooodles.com, betterit.com, ingeniotech.co.uk) plus a Reddit thread. Only
two Irish results. Our `/do-i-need-an-it-manager` page already carries real
Irish salary figures and a comparison table, which is exactly the
information gain the checklist asks for.

**`it support ennis clare`** — no serious MSP content at all. Page one is
local directories (locallyirish, connectedhubs, digiclare), a jobs board, and
two small local firms. The smallest market, and the only location SERP that
looks winnable on content alone.

**`how does IT affect business valuation when selling`** — **no MSP appears
anywhere on page one.** It is accountants and consultancies: acma.ie,
Deloitte, Investopedia, Commerce Bank, Reddit.

**`it due diligence acquisition ireland`** — big-four and law firms: EY,
Deloitte, BearingPoint, LexisNexis, Travelers. The only MSP is it.ie. Nobody
is serving owner-led businesses of 10–150 people on this.

## What this means for the strategy

The generic "IT support Dublin" fight is 8-deep against incumbents with years
of authority. We will not win it soon, and planning around it wastes the one
real advantage this business has.

**The owner-and-shareholder angle has no MSP competition in Ireland at all.**
Exit value, succession, what technology does to a valuation, IT due diligence
in an acquisition — these SERPs are occupied by accountants, consultancies
and US generalists, none of whom sell managed IT, and none of whom are
writing for an Irish owner of a 40-person distributor.

That is precisely the positioning the business already has, and the pages
already exist: `/exit-value`, `/succession`, `/ma-it-due-diligence`,
`/ma-cyber-due-diligence`, `/value-calculator`, `/own-vs-rent-compute`. They
were built as supporting content. **The SERP data says they should be the
spearhead**, with the location and vertical pages as the cluster beneath,
rather than the other way round.

The recommended shift, in one line: stop trying to out-rank nine incumbents
for the term they have owned for a decade, and own the question none of them
is answering — what technology does to what the business is worth.

## Still unknown

Absolute search volumes. Keyword Planner froze twice; the Bing Webmaster
Tools keyword tool is free and gives real figures, and is the next route to
try. Volume matters for sequencing but does not change the structural
conclusion above: a term with no qualified competition and modest volume
beats a term with high volume and nine entrenched incumbents, when the site
has no authority yet.
