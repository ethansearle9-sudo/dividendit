## Video 1: Why You Should Invest in Inbound Marketing

In all great stories, the concept of the hero's journey is simple. The beloved hero goes on a quest, faces untold difficulty, but rises victorious and returns home transformed. They have help along the way, of course. Often taking the form of a sage or wizard, there's usually someone who aids the hero on their adventure, providing guidance and advice. This guardian helps the hero grow to their fullest potential and achieve what they set out to do.

**When it comes to marketing, the role of the sage advisor falls to you.** Every prospect and customer is on a quest to accomplish a set of goals. They may not know where their journey with you will lead them quite yet, but as a marketer, it's your job to act as a guide. This means being ready for any of your visitors' questions or concerns when they need answers the most.

Why? In short, your help, education, and attention builds trust between you and your prospect or customer.

The question then becomes, how do you scalably interact with the multitude of buyers pursuing your site and marketing channels? How do you go beyond answering questions to empower and inspire so many potential buyers?

**Inbound marketing.**

> **Inbound marketing is a strategy that attracts and retains customers by creating valuable content and tailored experiences. Inbound marketing forms connections customers are looking for and solves problems they already have.** While outbound marketing interrupts your audience with content they don't always want, inbound marketing seamlessly integrates into a buyer's decision-making landscape.

Effective inbound marketing relies on successful implementation of the inbound methodology across all your marketing strategies.

### The inbound methodology — attract, engage, delight

> The inbound methodology is a method for growing an organization by building lasting relationships with people and helping them reach their goals. This is carried out in three stages: attract, engage, and delight.

**Attract.** You attract visitors with useful content and eliminate barriers as they learn about your company. **The key is to earn people's attention, not force it.** Applied to marketing: blogging, SEO, social publishing, paid search ads, and display advertising. Together these help you reach target buyers on the channels they're using to look for solutions, ways to achieve goals, or to browse new products and services.

> **Pro tip: Focus on the attract stage if your goal is to generate more traffic and leads.**

**Engage.** You make it easy for customers to shop and buy from you by enabling buyers to interact with you on their preferred timeline and channels. **Here you focus on opening relationships, not just closing deals.** Applied to marketing: website and email personalization, database segmentation, marketing automation, lead nurturing, and multichannel communication such as chat and messaging. These build trust with leads and deliver content at every stage of their decision-making process.

> **Pro tip: Focus on the engage stage if you are looking to convert more leads and prospects, or trying to lower the cost to acquire new customers.**

**Delight.** You help, support, and empower customers to reach their goals. **Here you focus on creating buzz around your brand.** Applied to marketing: social mentions, reviews, word-of-mouth marketing, a customer loyalty program, or user-generated content. Often this requires shifting resources so they're more effectively distributed throughout the entire customer experience. The payoff: more loyal customers and new referral traffic.

> **Pro tip: Focus on the delight stage if you're looking to improve customer retention, customer lifetime value, and referrals.**

### The four foundations that must be in place first

Effective implementation of any of these strategies at any stage requires a few processes in place. They act as guardrails and ensure that whatever tactic you use, your buyers are at the centre of everything you do.

**1. Buyer personas.** You don't want just any traffic to your site. You want the right traffic — the visitors most likely to become leads and, ultimately, happy customers.

> Buyer personas are created through research, analysis, and taking a close look at who's already buying from you. They help you get into the mindset of your potential buyers and create the right content. Buyer personas help you identify groups in your customer base that share similar demographics, psychographics, and behaviors. They reveal what these groups of visitors want to see, when to deliver it, and how to better service folks throughout their lifecycle with your business.

**2. The buyer's journey.** Throughout the buyer's journey, visitors overcome challenges and accomplish goals differently. You need to understand what they're thinking and — potentially more importantly — **feeling**, as they progress toward a purchase. That way you can match them step for step: from the **awareness** that a problem exists, to **consideration** of potential solutions, until they reach a **decision**.

**3. Channels and distribution.** Next you need to know their preferred platforms and methods of communication — where they'd like to engage online. Think critically about publishing and distribution channels. Consider how you're leveraging ads, engagement, and business pages on platforms like Facebook, TikTok, Instagram or Twitter. Are your buyers turning to search engines like Google for research? Consider your content strategy. Where are you currently ranking with content like pillar pages, blog posts, or video content?

This level of personalisation pays off:

- **Epsilon: 80% of consumers are more likely to make a purchase when brands offer personalized experiences.**
- **Adobe: 67% of consumers think it's important for brands to automatically adjust content based on current context.**
- **The same Adobe study: 42% of consumers will "get annoyed" if brands don't adjust their content to their needs.**

**4. Segmentation and automation.** Customers expect unique experiences — tailored recommendations, dynamic content, exclusive offers — usually configured on a visitor's behaviour on your website, their demographics and interests.

> Audience segmentation helps group your most important buyers based on behavioral and demographic data. Using that data, you can determine which of those audiences are most valuable and most receptive to certain kinds of messages, and therefore, which audiences you should focus on creating content for.

Effective marketing automation considers the evolving needs of your leads and the behaviours and interactions they have with you **across all your marketing channels — not just email**. Behavioural inputs from multiple channels, such as likes on social posts, views of your pricing page, or engagements with particular content, give you the context to fully understand a lead's challenges.

### Proving it works: marketing attribution

> Marketing attribution reports pull together all the relevant interactions from your buyers' journey using pre-built models that can definitively answer which channels and content are helping you meet your marketing goals.

They help you and your team get the credit you deserve. In the past it's been really difficult for marketing teams to prove their contribution to the company's bottom line. With attribution reports you can show how much influence your content had, which can help you advocate for more headcount and budget.

### The close

Before diving into all the strategies that can power your inbound marketing flywheel, you need to start with a strong foundation. By building your inbound marketing strategy with these pieces in place, you can make data-driven decisions, grow sustainably, and ensure your customer is at the heart of everything you do.

> **Inbound marketing is about making your customers more successful, building relationships by doing the right thing, and focusing on the long-term even when it's not the easiest path. By focusing on your customers, your business will grow better.**

## Video 2: Inbound Marketing in Practice

Inbound marketing is all about publishing helpful and interesting content your audience wants to consume. But with an infinite number of approaches and ever-increasing creative standards, designing campaigns can be overwhelming. Four worked examples.

### Blogging — M.M. LaFleur

Blogs help drive traffic to your website, convert that traffic into leads, establish authority in an industry, help your business grow, and **attract new customers months and even years after publication**.

M.M. LaFleur is a professional apparel brand. Their blog, *The — M — Dash*, promotes women's empowerment: gender equality, stereotypes, being a woman in the workplace, alongside attire posts (first day, casual Fridays, job interviews). The blog promotes education and discussion around women in the workplace while relating the content back to their clothing line. They discuss societal issues in a way that makes readers feel good, which leads them to feel good about the brand as a whole.

> **The takeaway: take time to understand the topics your audience really cares about.** Brainstorm ways to incorporate thought leadership pieces into your content strategy. This helps your brand build credibility in your online space over time.

### Social media — ASOS

ASOS sells its own line plus clothing from 850 brands, shipping to 196 countries. Instagram: over 12.2 million followers, over 14k posts, posting on average six times a day — edgy, colourful, inspirational, usually outfit inspiration, a celebrity wearing ASOS, or styling tips. When showcasing a product they add the product's serial number in the caption for easier access.

Brand creative director John Mooney: *"We're always evolving our content, social media strategy, and channel focus to ensure we're staying relevant to our fashion-loving, 20-something customers."*

The detail that matters: **the only link in their Twitter account description goes to their customer care page.** ASOS knows its personas will turn to Twitter for help, versus other platforms where they may be more interested in discovery or bonding.

> **Takeaways:** understand which platforms your audience finds interesting and relevant; your audience is diverse and spread out, so different channels create multiple points of contact which nurture leads and increase conversions. Create content that matches the voice and tone you want for your brand — **brand voice is critical for consistency across channels regardless of who creates the content**. And publish the type of content a user is looking for *on that specific channel*, because not all content performs the same everywhere.

### Email — JetBlue

Before you say email is dead: **OptinMonster found 99% of email users check their inbox every day, some 20 times a day. 58% of consumers check email first thing in the morning.**

Email as a tool isn't dead. Some email *practices* are — impersonal email address lines, violating GDPR, ignoring the user experience.

JetBlue delivers lovable marketing in cheeky campaigns that humorously re-engage customers. Every element, from headers to actionable, contrasting CTAs, works together to create an engaging email that is **promotional without being pushy**. The header is personalised to the recipient based on past flying and search history. The copy is bursting with friendly personality and airline jokes, and reads as though it comes from a friend, which earns a positive reaction.

> **JetBlue understands that every interaction counts.** Use a voice and tone your audience relates to, and ensure the content is relevant to them. To do this at scale, **think beyond just demographic segmentation and take past customer behavior into account**.

### Personalised outreach — Sock Club

Sock Club is a subscription service delivering socks, serving both businesses wanting personalised socks with their logo and individuals wanting socks featuring their pet. That's a lot of segments to handle at scale.

Through careful analysis of customer data, Sock Club found potential customers typically find them through organic or paid search, so they structure their entire content strategy around SEO best practices. Once a customer lands on the site they're directed to create an account to begin a personalised order — **streamlining the sign-up gathers critical personal information early**, which then helps personalise the experience. After a client uploads their logo, an account manager reaches out with customised quotes in-account or by email, and the lead passes to sales.

Here's where it gets interesting. Sock Club noticed that after an account had been created, leads and customers **didn't engage as much** with their content — so automated lead nurturing workflows promoting blog posts underperformed. The team turned to A/B testing.

> **What drives clicks and conversions for Sock Club: simple text emails personalised to look like they're coming from a lead or customer's account manager.**

This changed how Sock Club thinks about lead nurturing. They now automate outreach about upcoming sales, incentives and promotions without the bells and whistles of a traditional marketing email — because that's the experience their customers want. With tracking implemented along the way, they have data for every stage of their buyer's journey and sales pipeline, and can tell which leads are completing which actions.

> **Takeaway: personalization and automation can be your best friend.** They make certain strategies accessible even without a massive marketing team or budget. Testing and tracking how your content performs unlocks further insight about your audience.

### The close

This was by no means a comprehensive list. You'll also want to consider ads that help rather than interrupt, or videos that educate and are easily shareable.

> **The biggest takeaway is that what inbound looks like will depend on your audience, the type of content and experience that works best for them, and how you plan to scale it to achieve your short-term and long-term marketing goals.**

## Video 3: Your Inbound Marketing Toolkit

Now that you understand what inbound marketing can look like in practice, consider the tools, information and resources you need to create a strong inbound marketing foundation.

> **Just as you should build your inbound marketing strategies around your buyer personas, you should build your marketing toolkit around your customer data.**

### Start with a CRM

Any company that wants to maintain a relationship with their customers can benefit from a CRM. For most businesses their most valuable asset is their customers, and at most companies the details about those customers — who they are and how they've interacted with your organisation — are spread across many different places.

> **Your prospects and customers will notice the effects of a disjointed team. From their perspective, they have a relationship with one company, not a collection of different people and departments.** Everyone on your team needs context about every customer's needs, wants, and current state, so they can pick up the conversation where it left off.

A CRM provides one central place to organise all the details of your leads and customers, and makes it easy for everyone to gain insight into the state of the business and the status of every customer relationship.

### Then the martech stack

> A marketing technology — martech — stack is the list of tools that marketers use to execute elements of their marketing campaigns, from lead generation to email marketing, and from social media management to search engine optimization.

These tools are time-saving, as they typically streamline and automate your processes, and help you analyse the success of your efforts over time. One martech tool is typically used for each marketing discipline, though some platforms cover many tasks.

| Discipline | Tools named in the lesson |
|---|---|
| SEO | SEMrush, Moz, Ahrefs, Google Search Console |
| Content marketing | WordPress (build/maintain site), BuzzSumo (measure content success), Hotjar (track online behaviour), Canva (graphics) |
| Social media | Hootsuite, Buffer, Sprout Social |
| Advertising | AdRoll, RollWorks, 6sense, Adstream |
| Automation | HubSpot, Drip, Marketo, Omnisend |
| Reporting | Google Analytics, HubSpot, Databox |
| Collaboration | Slack |
| Project management | Trello, Asana, Airtable |

Instead of adopting a plethora of individual tools, some marketers adopt an all-in-one solution such as HubSpot to ensure their data stays connected.

> **Whichever platforms you choose, make sure you have at least one good automation feature in your toolbox.** This cuts down time wasted on daily tasks. A good automation tool doesn't just send drip sequences — it can trigger specific actions such as updating a contact record or adding a contact to a list when certain criteria are met.

### The close

> Between a CRM and a comprehensive martech stack that includes automation, reporting, collaboration, and project management features, you'll have the inbound marketing toolkit you need to **attract, engage, and delight your customers at scale**.
