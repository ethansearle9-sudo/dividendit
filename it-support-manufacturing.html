IT support for manufacturing

Most IT providers plan maintenance around their week. In manufacturing that is how you end up patching during a production run.

Why manufacturing is different

The constraint is not what you fix. It is when you are allowed to touch it.

A manufacturer's IT estate has two halves that rarely get treated as one. There is the office side, which looks like any other business. And there is everything near the floor: machine controllers, the PCs that drive them, scanners, label printers and the ERP that ties production to orders.

The second half is where generic IT support falls down. Those systems often run older software for good reasons, cannot simply be rebooted mid-run, and have maintenance windows dictated by production rather than by a support rota. A provider who patches on a schedule that suits them will eventually take a line down.

There is a second exposure that owners under-price: the drawings, designs, tooling specifications and process knowledge. That is often worth more than the machinery. If it is encrypted by ransomware, or copied on the way out of the building by someone leaving, the loss is permanent and it is competitive.

Manufacturing needs IT that is planned around the production calendar, and security that treats the design library as the asset it is.

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

Can you work around our production schedule?

Yes, and it is the first thing we agree. Your production calendar decides maintenance windows, written down rather than renegotiated each month. Nothing on or near the line gets touched mid-run without agreement.

We have machines running older software that cannot be updated. Can you help?

Yes, and that is normal in manufacturing. Where a system genuinely cannot be patched, the answer is to isolate it so a compromise elsewhere cannot reach it, control who can access it, and monitor it. Replacing it is rarely realistic and we will not pretend otherwise.

Is our intellectual property really at risk?

Yes, in two ways that look very different. Ransomware encrypts it, which is loud and obvious. Someone copying the design library on their way to a competitor is quiet, and in our experience owners worry far less about it than they should.

Do you cover shift patterns?

Yes. If you run two or three shifts, support needs to match, which is one of the clearest arguments for a team over a single hire. One person covers roughly a third of the week.

What does it cost?

Per user, per month, tools included, depending on headcount and what is already in place. Twenty minutes gives an honest number. Contracts roll monthly.
