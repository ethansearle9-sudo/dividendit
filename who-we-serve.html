Who We Serve

Who we serve

The argument for outsourcing IT is not the same in a factory as it is in a wealth management firm. These pages say what is actually different about each sector rather than repeating the same promises.

Why industry changes what good IT looks like

The technology underneath is largely the same from one business to the next. What changes is what an outage costs, which data carries an obligation, who is allowed to ask you hard questions about your security, and when in the year you cannot afford anything to go wrong.

Those four things decide how IT should be designed and when work can be done. An IT provider who does not know them will sell you a competent service that is wrong for your business, and you will only find out on your worst week.

We work mainly with distribution, manufacturing, financial services, medical and life sciences, and FMCG and food producers. Those are the industries where the question is not whether technology works, but whether it holds when the business is under load.

What every client gets, whatever the industry

The baseline does not move. Service desk, monitoring and patching, a security standard aligned to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, backup and recovery that has actually been tested, and a named person who owns the plan rather than just the tickets.

Pricing is per user, per month, with tools included, on a rolling monthly contract. That is the same whether you run a warehouse or a wealth management firm.

What changes from one industry to another

In distribution and wholesale, an outage is not an inconvenience: the order is not placed and the shelf stays empty. Work gets planned around the order cycle, not around our calendar.

In manufacturing, production cannot stop for a patch window, and the design library is the most valuable thing you own. Cover has to span shifts rather than office hours.

In financial services, a compromised mailbox is not an IT incident, it is a client trust incident, and you are judged on how fast you can prove what happened.

In medical and life sciences, patient data carries the highest obligation of anything we handle, and the clock on notifying a breach starts whether or not you have noticed it.

In FMCG and food, the retailer's calendar is the real calendar, seasonal peaks are unforgiving, and traceability has to survive an audit.

The pages below set out each of these honestly, including where we would tell you we are not the right fit.

If your industry is not listed

These are the industries we know best, not the only ones we work in. The test is not your sector, it is whether you have between 10 and 150 people, no real IT function of your own, and technology that has quietly become a risk rather than an advantage.

There are industries we do not take on, and we will say so early rather than waste your time.
