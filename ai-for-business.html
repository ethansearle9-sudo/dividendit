AI for business, without the leaks

AI tools such as Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT and Claude can take hours of routine work off your team every week. The risk is not the tool. It is what the tool can reach. We treat AI as one more workload: we choose the right tools, put your data permissions in order first, and set clear rules for use.

The problem

Your team is already using AI. The question is whether you know where your data is going.

In most businesses, AI arrived the same way every other tool did: one person at a time, on a free account, without anyone deciding anything. Someone pastes a customer list into a chatbot to tidy it up. Someone else uploads a contract to get a summary. It saves them an hour, so they keep doing it.

That hour is real, and it is worth having. But nobody has checked what those accounts keep, who else can see it, or what happens to it when that person leaves. And when a business-grade tool like Copilot is switched on, it can read everything the user has permission to open, including the files that were shared too widely years ago.

AI is not a separate project. It is another workload, and it needs the same things as every other workload: the right tool for the job, access that matches each role, and someone accountable for it.

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to use ChatGPT or Claude for business?

Yes, on a business plan with sensible rules. The risk with free personal accounts is that the business does not control the account or know what has been put into it. Business plans give you control of accounts and clear terms on how your data is handled.

What do we need to do before switching on Microsoft Copilot?

Tidy your permissions. Copilot can read anything the user is allowed to open, so files that were shared too widely become visible through it. Reviewing and tightening access first is the most important step.

Should we ban AI tools at work?

Usually not. A ban tends to push use onto personal accounts where you cannot see it. Choosing approved tools and setting clear rules gives you the benefit and keeps control.

Which AI tool should a small business use?

It depends on where the work happens. If your team lives in Outlook, Word, Excel and Teams, Copilot fits naturally. For writing, analysis and research, ChatGPT or Claude may suit better. Many businesses use more than one, each for the job it does best.

Do we need an AI policy?

Yes, but a short one. It should say which tools are approved, what information must never go into them, and who to ask when unsure. One page your team will actually read is better than twenty they will not.

Is AI part of your managed service?

Advice on AI is part of how we run your technology, and Microsoft 365 is included at every service level. The licence cost of Copilot or other AI tools depends on how many people need them, so we size it with you.
