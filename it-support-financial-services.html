Who We Serve

IT support for financial services and wealth

In a wealth or insurance business, a compromised mailbox is not an IT incident. It is a client trust incident, and it is the one you cannot undo.

A mailbox takeover used to redirect a client payment
How it works
Remote-first support with evidence kept for regulators and auditors
Why financial services is different

Your clients are not buying returns. They are buying the belief that you are careful.

The people who bring in revenue in a financial firm should never think about IT. When it works, it is invisible. When it does not, advisers stop advising and start waiting, and that is expensive in a business where the product is attention and judgement.

The more serious exposure is the mailbox. Client instructions, payment details and personal information move through it constantly. An attacker who gets in can read for weeks and then intervene once, at the moment a client is expecting to move money. The financial loss can usually be worked through. The damage to a client's belief that you are careful with their affairs is much harder to repair.

And this sector gets asked to show its work. Regulators, auditors, professional indemnity insurers and institutional partners all now ask detailed questions about access control, backups and incident response. Answering well is routine if the evidence exists and painful if it does not.

What matters in a financial services environment

✓

Mailbox protection, taken seriously

Multi-factor sign-in on every account without exception, conditional access rules, and alerting on the mailbox rule changes attackers use to hide.

✓

Payment instruction controls

A verification habit on any change to payment details, so no single email can move client money.

✓

Evidence kept current

Access reviews, backup tests and incident records retained, so a regulator, auditor or insurer question is answered from a folder.

✓

Client data handled properly

Access limited to who genuinely needs it, and leavers removed the day they leave.

✓

IT that stays invisible

Proactive support, so advisers are not waiting on a laptop.

✓

One predictable bill

Per user, per month, tools included, rolling monthly.

How it works

How we start with a financial firm

Secure identity first

Multi-factor sign-in everywhere, starting with directors, finance and anyone handling client instructions.

Agree the payment verification rule

Written down and applied without exception, because the exception is how the loss happens.

Get the evidence in order

Access reviews, backup testing and an incident plan, recorded rather than remembered.

Take over the day to day

Support, patching, monitoring, vendor chasing. Onboarded within 30 days.

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest IT risk for a wealth or broking firm?

Mailbox takeover used to redirect a payment. An attacker gets into an account, reads quietly until a client is expecting to transfer money, then intervenes with convincing timing. Multi-factor sign-in plus a verification habit on payment changes stops almost all of it.

Can you help with regulator and insurer questionnaires?

Yes. We make sure the controls being asked about are genuinely running and we keep the evidence, so completing one is administrative rather than a scramble. We are not compliance consultants and we do not certify anything, so where you need formal compliance advice we will say so.

Do you offer ISO 27001?

No. We do not offer ISO 27001 certification or readiness work. We structure security around the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and report against it, which covers most of what partners and insurers ask about.

Our advisers are often out of the office. Does that change anything?

It raises the stakes on identity rather than location. Working from anywhere is fine when sign-in is properly protected, devices are managed and access is controlled. It is a problem when any of those are missing.

What does it cost?

Per user, per month, tools included, depending on headcount and what is in place. Twenty minutes gives an honest number, and contracts roll monthly.
