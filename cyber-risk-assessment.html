The problem

Are we actually secure, or have we just been lucky?

It is a hard question to answer from the inside. Your IT person or provider will usually say things are fine, and they may be right. But they are marking their own work, and what they consider fine may not match what an insurer, a customer or an attacker would find.

Without an independent view, security spending tends to follow the last sales call or the last scare. Money goes on products while basic gaps stay open.

A risk assessment replaces guesswork with a ranked list. Every finding is rated by how likely it is and how much it would hurt the business, so you know what to fix first and what can wait.

What it's worth

Spend on the right things, in the right order

The assessment usually pays for itself by stopping one unnecessary purchase or by finding one gap before a criminal does.

  • It directs your budget. You invest in what reduces risk, not in what was sold most persuasively.
  • It prepares you for hard questions. Insurers, customers and buyers ask what you know about your own risk. Now you can show them.
  • It gives you a baseline. Repeat it later and you can prove the business is getting stronger.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • You have never had an independent review of your security
  • You are about to renew cyber insurance or answer a customer questionnaire
  • You are considering a change of IT provider
  • You are preparing for investment, acquisition or sale

Probably not the right fit if

  • You need proof that a specific system can withstand an attack. That is a penetration test
  • You want a rubber stamp. We will tell you what we find
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a risk assessment, an audit and a penetration test?

A risk assessment looks broadly at your controls and rates the gaps by business impact. An audit checks you against a specific standard. A penetration test has specialists actively try to break into defined systems. Most businesses should start with a risk assessment.

How long does a cyber risk assessment take?

It depends on the size and complexity of your business. We agree the timeline in the scoping call before any work starts, so there are no surprises.

Will you use it to sell us services?

The report and roadmap are yours, and you can take them to any provider or to your internal team. If you would like our help with the actions, we will quote for that separately.

What do you need from us?

A named contact, access to review your configurations and a little time from the people who run your systems. We keep the demands on your team light.
