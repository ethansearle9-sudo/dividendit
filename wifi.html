The problem

One router in a cupboard is not a WiFi network.

Most WiFi problems come from the same place: equipment designed for a home, placed wherever was convenient and expected to cover a whole business. The signal is strong beside the router and useless in the meeting room.

Adding more boxes without a plan often makes it worse. They interfere with each other, and devices cling to a distant one when a closer one is available.

Reliable WiFi is a design problem. It starts with measuring your building and ends with access points in the right places, managed as one system.

What it's worth

Connectivity your operations can rely on

In a warehouse or on a shop floor, a WiFi dead spot is not an annoyance. It is a scanner that stops scanning.

  • It keeps operations moving. Handheld scanners, tablets and tills stay connected where the work happens.
  • It improves every call and meeting. Video and voice depend on stable wireless more than on raw speed.
  • It protects the business. Guests and unmanaged devices are kept away from your systems.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • There are places in your building where WiFi does not work
  • Calls drop when people walk around
  • Guests use the same WiFi password as staff
  • You rely on wireless scanners, tablets or tills

Probably not the right fit if

  • You need WiFi for a home
  • You want a single access point posted to you without design or installation
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

Why is our office WiFi so slow?

The usual causes are too few access points, poor placement, interference from neighbouring networks or home-grade equipment handling more devices than it was built for. A survey identifies which applies to you.

What is a WiFi site survey?

It is a measured assessment of your premises showing signal strength, interference and dead spots, used to decide how many access points you need and where they should go.

Is guest WiFi a security risk?

It is when guests share the same network as your business systems. Properly separated guest access gives visitors the internet and nothing else.

Can you cover a warehouse or outdoor yard?

Yes. Warehouses, with high racking and metal, need careful design, and outdoor areas need suitable equipment. The survey accounts for both.
