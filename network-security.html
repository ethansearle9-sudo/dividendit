Network security: a managed firewall and a network with walls in it

Your network connects everything you own. We design it so that a problem in one place stays in one place, and we keep the front door managed and watched. A managed firewall, a network split into zones, secure WiFi and controlled remote access, so a break-in cannot walk through the rest of the business.

The problem

Most office networks are one big open room.

In a typical small business network, everything can talk to everything: the finance PC, the guest WiFi, the CCTV, the smart TV in the boardroom and the server. It was the easiest way to set it up, and it means one compromised device has a path to all the others.

The firewall is often a box that was installed years ago and never looked at again. Its software is out of date, the rules have grown by accretion and nobody reviews what it is logging.

Good network security is not about buying a bigger box. It is about walls inside the network, a front door that is maintained, and someone checking both.

What it's worth

Containment is what limits the damage

You cannot guarantee nothing will ever get in. You can decide in advance how far it is able to go.

  • It limits the cost of an incident. A contained problem is a bad afternoon. An uncontained one can stop the whole business.
  • It protects operations. Warehouse scanners, production equipment and tills keep working because they are separated from office risks.
  • It reduces key-person risk. A documented network can be supported by a team, not just the person who built it.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • Your firewall was installed and has not been reviewed since
  • Guests, staff and devices all share one network
  • You have operational equipment such as scanners, machinery or cameras on the network
  • People connect remotely to systems in the office

Probably not the right fit if

  • Everyone works from home on cloud applications with no office network. Identity and endpoint security matter more for you
  • You want hardware supplied without ongoing management
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What does a managed firewall service include?

Configuration, software updates, rule reviews, monitoring and changes when your business needs them. The firewall is treated as a living control, not a box installed once.

What is network segmentation?

Dividing a network into separate zones so that devices in one zone cannot freely reach devices in another. It means a compromised guest device or camera cannot reach your finance system.

Do we still need a firewall if everything is in the cloud?

If you have an office, yes, although its job changes. It protects the devices in the building and controls what connected equipment can reach. For fully remote teams, identity and device security carry more of the weight.

Is a VPN still the right way to work remotely?

Sometimes. For access to systems in your office, a properly configured VPN tied to multi-factor sign-in works well. For cloud applications, conditional access on the sign-in is usually simpler and safer.
