Penetration testing: find the gaps before someone else does

A penetration test is the closest thing to a real attack you can order on purpose. Independent testers try to break in, then show you exactly how and what to fix.

The problem

Controls on paper are not the same as controls that hold.

You can have a firewall, protected devices and policies in a folder and still be exposed by one forgotten test server, one weak password on an old account or one web form that trusts what it is given.

The only way to know whether your defences work is to have someone try to get past them, using the same techniques a criminal would, with your permission and within agreed limits.

A good test does not end with a long list of technical findings. It tells you what an attacker could actually reach, what that would mean for the business and what to fix first.

What's included

Types of penetration testing

✓

External network testing

What an attacker on the internet can find and exploit: exposed services, weak sign-in and misconfiguration.

✓

Internal network testing

What someone could do from inside, after a phished password or a compromised laptop: how far they could move and what they could reach.

✓

Web application and API testing

Testing of your web applications, customer portals and the interfaces behind them against the well-known classes of weakness.

✓

Cloud and Microsoft 365 review

Checks of how your cloud services are configured, where a single wrong setting can expose a great deal.

✓

Wireless testing

Whether your WiFi can be used as a way in, and whether guest access is as separate as you think.

✓

Social engineering

Controlled phishing and pretext calls to test whether your people and processes hold up under pressure.

How it works

How a penetration test runs

Scope

We agree what will be tested, when, and the rules that keep your operations safe.

Test

Independent testers carry out the work, telling you immediately about anything critical.

Report

You receive findings ranked by real-world risk, each with a clear fix, plus a summary for leadership.

Retest

Once fixes are in, the issues are tested again so you can show they are closed.

What it's worth

Evidence that your defences work

More and more, someone outside the business wants proof. A penetration test report, with the fixes closed out, is that proof.

  • It satisfies customers and tenders. Larger customers increasingly ask suppliers for a recent independent test.
  • It finds what scanning misses. Human testers chain small weaknesses together the way real attackers do.
  • It supports a sale. A clean, recent report answers a buyer's question before it is asked.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • A customer, insurer or auditor has asked for a penetration test
  • You have launched or changed a web application or portal
  • You want to prove last year's security investment worked
  • You are preparing the business for due diligence

Probably not the right fit if

  • You have never reviewed your security at all. A risk assessment is better value first
  • You want an automated vulnerability scan only. That is a different, lighter exercise
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is penetration testing?

It is an authorised, controlled attempt to break into your systems using the methods a real attacker would use, in order to find and fix weaknesses before they are exploited.

How often should a business have a penetration test?

Commonly once a year and after any significant change, such as a new application, a new office or a move to a new platform. Our Grow service level includes an annual independent test.

Will the test disrupt our business?

It should not. The scope and timing are agreed beforehand, risky techniques are avoided on live systems, and you have a direct contact throughout.

Who carries out the testing?

Specialist testers who are independent of the team that manages your IT day to day. Independence is what gives the result its credibility.

How do I choose a penetration testing provider?

Look for independence from whoever built or runs the systems, a clear written scope, findings ranked by business risk and not just technical severity, and a retest included in the price.
