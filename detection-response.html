Managed detection and response

Prevention fails sometimes. What matters then is how quickly someone notices and what they do next. That is the whole job of detection and response.

The problem

The warning is usually there. Nobody is looking at it.

When we look back at incidents in smaller businesses, the signs were almost always visible beforehand: an inbox rule created at midnight, a sign-in from an unexpected country, security software switched off on one machine.

Those signals sit in logs and dashboards across a dozen systems. In most businesses nobody has the time to look, least of all at night or at the weekend, which is exactly when attackers prefer to work.

Detection and response puts the signals in one place and puts people in front of them. It is the difference between finding out in minutes and finding out from a supplier asking where their payment went.

What it's worth

Speed is what you are buying

The cost of an incident rises with every hour it goes unnoticed. Detection and response is how you buy those hours back.

  • It shrinks the damage. An account locked in minutes is an inconvenience. One used for three weeks is a fraud.
  • It supports notification duties. Knowing what happened and when is what makes regulatory and customer notifications possible.
  • It gives you a record. A documented incident history, with responses, is far stronger in due diligence than no history at all.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • Nobody is looking at your security alerts outside office hours
  • You move money or hold sensitive data and would be hurt by a slow discovery
  • Your insurer asks about monitoring and response
  • You want a named team to call when something looks wrong

Probably not the right fit if

  • The basics are not yet in place. Start with sign-in protection and endpoint security

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between MDR, SIEM and a SOC?

A SIEM is the system that collects and correlates logs. A SOC, or security operations centre, is the team of people who watch it. MDR, managed detection and response, is the service that gives you both, plus the action taken when something is found.

How quickly do you respond to an alert?

Response targets are set out in your service agreement and reported on each month. Well-understood threats are contained automatically within minutes.

What happens during an incident?

We contain the threat, investigate how it happened and what was affected, and keep one named person on your side informed throughout. Afterwards you receive a plain-English report. Our first 24 hours guide shows the full sequence.

Do we still need cyber insurance if we have this?

Yes. Monitoring reduces the likelihood and the size of an incident. Insurance helps with the costs if one happens. Insurers also look favourably on businesses that have monitoring in place.
