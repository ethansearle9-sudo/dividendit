The Winchester problem

You are close enough to London to be charged like London, and far enough away to be visited like the provinces.

Winchester has a particular commercial profile: a lot of professional services, independent financial and wealth firms, and specialist businesses that are small in headcount and serious in value. The kind of business where ten people generate revenue that would need fifty elsewhere.

The IT market around it does not match that well. Larger providers price from London and treat Hampshire as an outlying territory, so you pay metropolitan rates and still wait for a site visit. The alternative is a very good local individual, which works until they are ill, on holiday, or busy with another client during your bad week.

There is also a quieter issue. Professional and financial firms here hold client money instructions and personal data, which makes them worth attacking regardless of size. Being a well-regarded twelve-person firm in Hampshire is not camouflage.

What fits is a team that works remotely as the default, responds in minutes rather than days, and turns up when it genuinely matters.

Winchester has its own page because the local argument is different here. The wider picture is on our outsourced IT support across Ireland, the UK and South Africa page.

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

Do you actually come on site in Winchester?

Yes. Most issues are resolved remotely and considerably faster that way, but when hardware needs hands we come out, across Winchester and Hampshire towards Basingstoke, Eastleigh and Southampton.

We are a small professional firm. Are we too small?

If you have roughly ten or more people and downtime costs you chargeable hours, no. Below that a full managed service is often more than you need, and we will say so rather than sell it.

Why not just use a London provider?

Plenty do, and some are very good. The two things to check are what you are paying for a postcode rather than a service, and what actually happens when you need someone physically present. Ask for the site visit commitment in writing.

Are Hampshire firms really targeted by cyber crime?

Yes, and mostly not deliberately. Attacks are automated and opportunistic, and professional and financial firms are attractive because of what moves through their mailboxes. Invoice fraud and mailbox takeover are far more common than ransomware.

What does it cost?

Per user, per month, tools included, depending on headcount and what is in place. Twenty minutes gives an honest number, and contracts roll monthly.
