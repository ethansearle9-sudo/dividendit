How to tell if your business has been hacked

Most businesses do not find out they have been compromised from a ransom note. They find out weeks later, from a supplier asking why they have not been paid. These are the warning signs that show up earlier, and how to check for them.

By Ethan Searle, Founder·8 min read·Updated November 2026

In this article

  1. Why compromise is usually quiet
  2. Signs in email
  3. Signs on computers and servers
  4. Signs in money and accounts
  5. A 15-minute check any owner can run
  6. Why someone needs to be watching

Why compromise is usually quiet

Modern attackers rarely announce themselves. If they get into a mailbox, the valuable thing is not to break it but to watch it: who pays whom, when, and how. If they get into a network, they spend time finding the backups and the most important data before doing anything visible.

That means the question "how do I know if our systems have been compromised?" is really "what small signs do attackers leave while they are watching?" Here are the twelve we see most.

Signs in email

  1. Inbox rules nobody created. Rules that move emails containing words like "invoice", "payment" or "bank" to an obscure folder, or forward copies to an outside address. This is the most reliable sign of mailbox takeover.
  2. Sent emails nobody remembers sending, or replies to conversations the mailbox owner has never seen.
  3. Multi-factor sign-in prompts nobody asked for. If someone receives approval requests out of the blue, their password is probably already known.
  4. Password reset or "new sign-in" alerts from Microsoft, Google, banks or other services that the person did not trigger.

Signs on computers and servers

  1. Security software switched off or uninstalled. Attackers disable protection before they act. It should never switch itself off.
  2. New programs, especially remote access tools, that nobody installed. Legitimate remote support software is a favourite way for attackers to keep access.
  3. Files that will not open or have strange new extensions. This can be the start of ransomware encrypting data.
  4. Unusual activity at odd hours: servers busy at 3am, large amounts of data leaving the network, a machine that is suddenly slow with its fan running hard.

Signs in money and accounts

  1. A supplier says they have not been paid for an invoice you paid. Check where the money went before anything else.
  2. Customers say they received new bank details from you. If you did not send them, your mailbox or your domain is being used against you.
  3. New user accounts, especially administrators, or existing accounts given more access than before.
  4. Sign-ins from unexpected countries or at impossible times, such as the same user in Dublin and Lagos within an hour.

A 15-minute check any owner can run

If you use Microsoft 365, you or your IT provider can check the most important signs today. Google Workspace has equivalents.

  • Inbox rules: in Outlook, open Settings, then Mail, then Rules, for directors and anyone in finance. Anything you do not recognise is a red flag.
  • Forwarding: in the same settings, check that email is not being forwarded to an outside address.
  • Sign-in methods: at mysignins.microsoft.com, check the phone numbers and authenticator apps registered against each finance and director account.
  • Recent sign-ins: the same page shows recent sign-in locations. Look for countries you do not operate in.
  • Admin accounts: ask your IT provider for a list of everyone with administrator access, and question every name.

Found something? Do not delete it yet. Take screenshots, then follow what to do in the first 24 hours.

Why someone needs to be watching

Every one of these twelve signs shows up in logs and alerts long before a supplier calls about a missing payment. The problem in most smaller businesses is not that the warning is missing, it is that nobody is looking at it, especially outside office hours.

That is the job of detection and response: tooling that collects the signals from email, devices and accounts, and people who look at the alerts around the clock and act within minutes. It is the difference between finding out in an hour and finding out in a month.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my computer system has been compromised?

Look for security software being disabled, programs nobody installed, files that will not open, unusual activity at odd hours, and in email, inbox rules or forwarding nobody set up. Any one of these deserves an immediate check by your IT or security provider.

What is the most common sign of a hacked business email account?

An inbox rule that hides or forwards messages about invoices or payments. Attackers create them so they can watch payment conversations without the account owner noticing replies.

Can a business be hacked without anyone noticing?

Yes, and it is common. Attackers often stay hidden for days or weeks to learn how the business pays suppliers or where its data and backups are. Continuous monitoring is what shortens that window.

Who should I call if I find signs of a hack?

Your IT or security provider first, and your bank immediately if any payment could be affected. If you have cyber insurance, call the incident line on the policy as well.
