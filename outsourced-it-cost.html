How much does outsourced IT cost?

Almost every provider answers this with “it depends” and then asks for a meeting. Here is what it actually depends on, so you can work out your own number before you talk to anyone: your user count, whether you run servers, how much security depth you need, and how much is already in place.

How the pricing actually works

Per user, per month. The question is what that user price contains.

Managed IT is sold per user, per month. That part is near-universal. What differs enormously between providers is what sits inside the number, and that is where two quotes that look like they are for the same thing turn out not to be.

The honest drivers are straightforward. How many people you have, because the per-user price usually falls as headcount rises. Whether you run servers, since physical or virtual servers add real work that user counts do not capture. How much security you want: a basic protective baseline costs considerably less than round-the-clock monitored detection and response. Whether Microsoft licensing is included or billed separately, which alone can move a quote by a third. And what state things are in now, because an environment that has been neglected for five years takes work to stabilise before it becomes routine.

What should not drive it is a long contract. A provider confident in the service does not need to lock you in for three years to deliver it.

Price only means something next to what you get for it. That is set out on our outsourced IT services page.

What's included

What to check is included before you compare prices

Two quotes are only comparable once you know these answers for both.

✓

Is licensing in or out?

Microsoft 365 licensing is a significant cost. A quote that includes it and one that does not are not comparable, and the gap is often around a third.

✓

Are the security tools included?

Endpoint protection, email security, backup and monitoring should be in the per-user price. If a provider sells you a tool and then charges separately for anyone to watch it, you are buying software, not a service.

✓

Is anyone actually watching?

Detection without response is a dashboard. Ask who looks at alerts, when, and what they are empowered to do at 3am.

✓

What is out of scope?

Projects, migrations, hardware and on-site visits are often quoted separately. Fair enough, but you want to know before, not after.

✓

Is there an onboarding fee?

Some charge it, some fold it in. Neither is wrong. Not knowing is.

✓

What is the term?

Rolling monthly versus a twelve or thirty-six month commitment materially changes what you are agreeing to.

How it works

How to work out your own number

Count users, not devices

A person with a laptop and a phone is one user. This is the figure every quote keys off.

Count servers separately

Physical or virtual, on site or hosted. These are priced on top of users almost everywhere.

Decide your security level

Protective baseline, or monitored detection and response around the clock. This is the single biggest lever on the number.

Decide on licensing

If you want Microsoft 365 bundled, say so when asking, so every quote is built the same way.

What it's worth

What a fair arrangement looks like

Price is only half of it. These are the terms that tell you how confident a provider is.

  • One number that includes the toolsYou should not be buying a service and then separately buying the things that make it work.
  • No lock-inRolling monthly says the provider expects to keep earning it. Annual terms are reasonable if they come with a discount and you choose them.
  • Clear scope boundariesWhat is included and what is a project, agreed in advance and in writing.
  • Reporting you can readA monthly account of what happened and what it meant, not a dashboard you have to interpret.

Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

How much does outsourced IT cost per user per month?

It is priced per user, per month, and the range is wide because what sits inside the number varies so much. The honest drivers are headcount, whether you run servers, how deep the security goes, whether Microsoft licensing is bundled, and the state of the environment today. Anyone quoting a precise figure without knowing those is guessing, and anyone refusing to explain the drivers is stalling.

Why will nobody publish a price?

Partly because the drivers genuinely vary, and partly because a meeting is easier to sell than a number. The drivers are not a secret, which is why they are set out on this page. You should be able to narrow your own range before speaking to anyone.

Is Microsoft 365 licensing usually included?

Sometimes, and it matters enormously. Licensing is a large component, so a quote that bundles it will look much higher than one that does not for identical service. Ask every provider to quote the same way or you are comparing nothing.

Should I pay an onboarding fee?

It is reasonable. Taking over an environment properly, documenting it and fixing what is wrong is real work. What is not reasonable is discovering it after you have agreed the monthly figure.

What should I avoid?

Three things. A long lock-in with no discount attached. Security tools sold without anybody monitoring them. And a quote so much cheaper than the others that something has clearly been left out, which is usually the monitoring or the licensing.
