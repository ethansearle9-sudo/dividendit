How the spend is structured, and how much of it is duplicated

How this is evidenced: supplier invoices before and after, licence tenancy export, purchase order for the perpetual estate. An accountant can tie every line to a bank statement.

Worth knowing: capitalising the perpetual purchase moves the cost below the EBITDA line as depreciation, which flatters EBITDA in isolation. The genuine saving is the cash difference over the four year life, which is what is shown here. A buyer's quality of earnings review will normalise the rest, so we do not count it.
02
Productivity

Availability and responsiveness, converted at your own margin

Modelled · assumption led
—
Unplanned downtime avoided, 38 hrs 38 × people × contribution/hr × realisation —
Slow systems and waiting, 8 min per person per day 8min × people × 220 days × contribution/hr × realisation —
Modelled effect—
What is measured: outage minutes from monitoring, ticket volume and handle time, device boot and logon telemetry. These are hard numbers.

What is assumed: that recovered time turns into output. It does not, entirely, which is what the realisation factor is for. Drop it to 40% and the figure moves with it. We would rather show you the dial than hide it inside a total.

Why contribution and not revenue: an hour returned to your team produces the margin on that hour's work, not the top line. Valuing it at revenue overstates the number by roughly four times.

Security

What the market actually charges to carry your risk

Banked · market priced
—
Cyber insurance premium, repriced on verified controls €8,400 renewal less €6,300 quoted —
Banked cash effect—
Why only the premium: a breach that did not happen cannot be priced honestly. Anyone showing you "€400,000 of breach cost avoided" is multiplying a borrowed probability by a borrowed impact and presenting the result as income. The premium delta is different: it is a real price, quoted by an underwriter who has seen the evidence pack. That is the one security number that belongs in a P&L conversation.
