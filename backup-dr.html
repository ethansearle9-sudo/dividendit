Backup and disaster recovery

Ransomware, a failed server, a deleted folder. The question is never whether you have a backup. It is whether you can restore the business, and how long it takes.

The problem

An untested backup is a hope, not a plan.

Almost every business has a backup of some kind. Far fewer have ever restored from it. The first real test tends to happen on the worst day, with the business stopped and everyone watching.

Attackers know this too. In a ransomware attack, one of the first things criminals look for is the backups, so they can delete them before locking everything else. A backup they can reach is a backup you cannot rely on.

Recovery is a business question before it is a technical one: how much data can you afford to lose, and how long can you afford to be down? The answers decide how backup should be designed.

What it's worth

The cheapest insurance you will buy

Recovery is the control that decides whether a serious incident is a bad week or the end of the business.

  • It gives you a choice in a ransomware attack. With clean, protected backups you can recover without negotiating with criminals.
  • It protects revenue. Every hour of downtime has a cost. A tested plan makes that cost known and limited.
  • It is asked about. Insurers and buyers both ask whether backups are separate, protected and tested. You will have the evidence.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • You cannot say when a restore was last tested
  • Your Microsoft 365 data has no separate backup
  • Your backups sit on the same network as everything else
  • A day without your systems would cost you serious money

Probably not the right fit if

  • You want storage space only, with no management or testing
  • You need a personal backup for a home computer
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

Doesn't Microsoft back up our Microsoft 365 data?

Microsoft keeps the service running, but protecting your data against accidental deletion, a departing employee or ransomware is your responsibility. Recycle bins and retention settings have time limits and are not a substitute for a separate backup.

What do RTO and RPO mean?

Recovery time objective is how long you can be down before it really hurts. Recovery point objective is how much recent data you could afford to lose. We agree both with you in plain terms and design the backup to meet them.

How often do you test restores?

On an agreed schedule, and the results are reported to you. Our Grow service level includes quarterly restore testing and disaster recovery planning.

Is cloud backup enough on its own?

It can be, if the copies are protected from deletion and you have tested how long a full restore takes over your internet connection. For large systems, a local copy alongside the cloud copy makes recovery much faster.
