CIO as a Service: senior technology leadership, part-time

The problem

Who in your business thinks about technology the way you think about the business?

In most companies of 20 to 150 people, nobody does. The IT provider keeps things running. The finance director signs off the invoices. The big decisions, such as which system to replace, whether to move to the cloud or how to use AI safely, get made by whoever has the time, often on a supplier's advice.

Suppliers are not neutral. Each one has something to sell and a reason to recommend an upgrade.

A CIO's job is to sit on your side of the table: to connect technology decisions to the business plan, challenge the suppliers and tell the board the truth.

What it's worth

Leadership pays for itself in decisions

One avoided mistake on a major system or contract usually covers the cost of a virtual CIO many times over.

  • Better decisions. Each major technology choice is tested against the business plan before money is committed.
  • Lower costs. Someone on your side reads the contracts and questions the renewals.
  • A stronger story at exit. A business with visible technology leadership and a documented plan is a lower-risk purchase.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • You are growing and technology decisions are getting bigger
  • Nobody at leadership level owns technology
  • You are unsure whether your suppliers' advice serves you or them
  • Investors or the board are asking questions about technology risk

Probably not the right fit if

  • You need hands-on daily IT support. That is managed IT
  • You already have a strong internal technology leader
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is a virtual CIO?

A virtual CIO, or vCIO, is a senior technology leader who works with your business part-time. They provide the strategy, oversight and board-level advice of a chief information officer without the cost of a full-time executive.

What is the difference between a virtual CIO and a fractional CIO?

In practice, very little. Both describe a part-time senior technology leader. Fractional sometimes implies a fixed number of days, and virtual implies a service delivered alongside managed IT.

How is this different from our IT provider's account manager?

An account manager looks after the relationship with one supplier. A CIO works for you, across all of your technology and suppliers, and is measured on business outcomes.

How much time do we get?

It is shaped around your needs: typically a quarterly strategy review, attendance at board or leadership meetings when useful, and access between them for decisions that cannot wait.
