Identity and access management

Most break-ins today do not break anything. Someone simply logs in with a stolen password. Identity and access management makes sure that is never enough.

The problem

A password is no longer protection.

Passwords get stolen every day, through fake login pages, reused details from other websites, or a convincing phone call. Once an attacker has one, they have the mailbox, the files and often the finance system that goes with it.

The answer is not longer passwords or more frequent changes. It is making sure that knowing the password is not enough: the person also has to prove who they are, from a device and a place you expect.

Done well, this is nearly invisible to your team. Done badly, or left half-finished with a few accounts excluded, it gives a false sense of security. Attackers look for the one account that was left out.

Enforced on every account for email, remote access and business applications, with no quiet exceptions.

✓

Phishing-resistant sign-in for key people

Passkeys or security keys for directors, finance and administrators, which do not work on fake login pages.

✓

Conditional access

Rules that only allow sign-in from managed devices and expected locations, and block risky attempts automatically.

✓

Single sign-on

One secure login for your applications. Fewer passwords for your team to manage and fewer for anyone to steal.

✓

Privileged access management

Administrator accounts kept few, separate from everyday accounts, tightly controlled and logged.

✓

Joiners, movers and leavers

Access granted on day one, adjusted when roles change and removed the day someone leaves, with regular reviews.

What it's worth

The control that pays for itself first

If you only fix one thing this quarter, fix sign-in. It closes the route behind most fraud and it is the first thing insurers and buyers ask about.

  • It blocks the most expensive incidents. Mailbox takeover is where invoice fraud starts. Strong sign-in stops it at the door.
  • It strengthens your insurance position. Being able to say that multi-factor sign-in covers every account, truthfully, matters at renewal and at claim time.
  • It is evidence in a sale. A clean record of who has access to what, and proof that leavers were removed, is exactly what a buyer's adviser wants to see.
Is it right for you?

An honest fit check

A good fit if

  • Your business runs on Microsoft 365 or other cloud applications
  • You are not certain every account has multi-factor sign-in
  • Former staff or old suppliers may still have access
  • Your insurer has started asking detailed questions about sign-in

Probably not the right fit if

  • You want a password manager only. We can help, but it is a small part of the answer
  • You need a consumer or family solution rather than a business one
Questions owners ask

Frequently asked questions

What is identity and access management?

It is the set of controls that decide who can sign in to your systems, how they prove who they are, and what they can reach once they are in. It includes multi-factor sign-in, conditional access, single sign-on and the management of administrator accounts.

Is multi-factor authentication enough on its own?

It stops the large majority of password attacks, but the most common forms, such as text codes and push approvals, can be bypassed by modern phishing. That is why we add phishing-resistant methods for high-risk people and conditional access for everyone. Our guide to email interception explains how.

What is privileged access management?

It is the practice of tightly controlling administrator accounts: keeping them few, separate from the accounts people use for email, protected by the strongest sign-in methods and fully logged.

Will this slow my team down?

It should do the opposite. Single sign-on means fewer logins, and on a recognised company device most people are rarely prompted. The friction lands on the attacker.
